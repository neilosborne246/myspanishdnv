Where to Live in Spain

Best places to live in Spain — a practical 2026 guide for expats

From Madrid's energy to Valencia's value and Malaga's booming nomad scene — here is an honest, city-by-city guide to where expats actually thrive in Spain.

By the My Spanish DNV team

Published: July 2026 Updated: regularly Read time: 14 min Category: Where to Live in Spain

Choosing where to live in Spain is one of the most consequential decisions you will make when planning your move. Spain is an enormous and deeply varied country — a professional in their 30s looking for a vibrant tech scene has very different needs from a family seeking good schools and green space, or a retiree prioritising warmth and a quiet social life. This guide covers the cities and areas that expats genuinely recommend in 2026, with honest assessments of cost, climate, and community.

What makes a great place to live in Spain?

When expats assess where to put down roots in Spain, the factors that consistently matter most are:

  • Cost of living — rent in particular, which varies by a factor of nearly three between the cheapest and most expensive cities
  • Climate — sunshine hours, winter temperatures, and humidity all vary substantially across Spain
  • Transport — both internal (getting around the city and travelling within Spain) and international (airport connections for visiting family and clients)
  • Expat community — the size and character of the English-speaking community matters significantly for settling in
  • Internet and coworking — critical for digital nomads and remote workers; Spain's fibre infrastructure is excellent nationwide but coworking density varies
  • Healthcare — both public and private options, and the accessibility of English-speaking doctors

Madrid — the capital's energy and opportunity

Madrid is Spain's capital and its largest city. For expats who need the full range of urban amenities — world-class museums, international business connections, a deep pool of English-speaking professionals, and a nightlife culture that is genuinely unlike anything else in Europe — Madrid is unmatched.

The city has a strong job market by Spanish standards, particularly in finance, consulting, technology, and international business. The expat community is large and well-organised, and English is widely spoken in professional environments.

The trade-off is cost. A one-bedroom apartment in a desirable central neighbourhood (Salamanca, Chamberí, Malasaña, Chueca) costs €1,300–1,800 per month in rent. A comfortable life for a single person in Madrid runs to approximately €1,800–2,400 per month including rent, depending on lifestyle. For a couple or family, budget upwards of €3,000 per month for a comfortable but not extravagant lifestyle.

Madrid's climate is also worth noting: hot, dry summers (frequently above 35°C in July and August) and cold winters (frequently below 5°C in January). It is emphatically not the warm, sunny year-round Spain of the tourist brochures — it sits at 650m altitude on a central plateau. That said, sunshine hours are excellent and snow is rare.

Barcelona — architecture, beach, and the highest costs in Spain

Barcelona is one of the most beautiful and internationally famous cities in Europe, and it attracts an enormous number of international residents as a result. The combination of extraordinary architecture (Gaudí, the Gothic Quarter, the Eixample), a beach within the city limits, a buzzing food and culture scene, and strong international business connections makes it uniquely appealing.

For expats, Barcelona is the most cosmopolitan Spanish city — the international community is dense, English is widely spoken in professional settings, and the city attracts a disproportionate share of European startup activity and creative industry.

The cost, however, is the highest in Spain. Rent for a one-bedroom apartment in central Barcelona runs €1,400–2,000 per month. Total monthly living costs for a single person approach €2,200–2,800 including rent. Families looking for spacious accommodation near a good school will find Barcelona stretches budgets considerably.

Catalonia's ongoing political complexities around independence are worth being aware of as a long-term resident — though for most expats the practical day-to-day impact is limited. Bilingualism (Spanish and Catalan) is the norm, and while Spanish works everywhere, some willingness to engage with Catalan culture goes a long way with local neighbours.

Valencia — the best-value major city in Spain

Valencia is consistently described by expats as the best overall place to live in Spain for those not constrained to a specific city for work. It is Spain's third-largest city — large enough to offer everything a major city should, small enough that it does not feel overwhelming or anonymous.

The case for Valencia is built on an exceptional combination of factors: 300 days of sun per year, a beach 20 minutes by public transport from the city centre, one of the best food cultures in the world (Valencia is the birthplace of paella and has an extraordinary fresh food market culture), excellent and affordable public transport, and a growing international community.

Cost of living is one of Valencia's greatest assets. Rent for a one-bedroom apartment in a desirable central neighbourhood (Ruzafa, El Carmen, Eixample) runs €700–1,000 per month. A comfortable life for a single person costs approximately €1,200–1,600 per month including rent — less than half the equivalent cost in London, New York, or Sydney.

For digital nomads specifically, Valencia is outstanding. Fibre broadband is excellent and cheap (€30–50 per month for fast fibre). Coworking spaces are numerous and well-priced. The city has direct flights to major European hubs, making client visits manageable. And the lifestyle — coffee working in a neighbourhood bar, lunch by the sea, evening tapas — is exactly what most people imagining the Spain life are actually imagining.

Seville — Andalusian character and genuine affordability

Seville is Spain's cultural jewel — the home of flamenco, the Semana Santa processions, extraordinary tapas, and some of the most beautiful historic architecture in Europe. It is also genuinely affordable, with rent for a one-bedroom apartment in a pleasant neighbourhood running €650–900 per month.

The challenge is the summer heat. Seville regularly exceeds 40°C in July and August — genuinely brutal temperatures that push many expats away for the peak summer months. From September through May, however, the climate is among the best in mainland Spain: warm, sunny, and mild. Many expats who choose Seville plan short summer escapes to the coast, treating the heat as a feature rather than a problem.

Seville has a smaller expat community than Valencia, Malaga, or Alicante, which means a richer immersion in Spanish culture — and a requirement to develop Spanish language skills relatively quickly. For expats who want to truly integrate rather than live in an English-language bubble, Seville is one of the best choices in Spain.

Malaga — Spain's fastest-growing nomad city

Malaga has undergone a remarkable transformation over the past decade. Once known primarily as the airport gateway to the Costa del Sol, it is now one of the most talked-about cities in Spain for digital nomads, tech workers, and international professionals. The Málaga Tech Park (PTA Málaga) has attracted significant investment, the city has invested heavily in infrastructure, and a wave of coworking spaces, international restaurants, and creative businesses has transformed formerly overlooked neighbourhoods.

The city has 320 days of sun per year, a genuinely beautiful historic centre, excellent connections via its international airport, and a cost of living that sits between Valencia's affordability and Barcelona's expense. Rent for a one-bedroom apartment in a central neighbourhood runs approximately €850–1,200 per month — more expensive than Valencia but significantly cheaper than Barcelona or Madrid.

Malaga's international airport provides direct connections to dozens of European cities, making it one of the best-connected cities in Spain for frequent travellers. The coast is immediately accessible — the beach at La Malagueta is a 15-minute walk from the historic centre.

Bilbao — Basque Country and world-class living standards

Bilbao is a city that surprises nearly every expat who visits. The Basque Country's largest city is consistently ranked among the highest quality-of-life cities in Spain — extraordinary food (the Basque Country has more Michelin stars per capita than almost anywhere on earth), a beautifully regenerated waterfront, the Guggenheim Museum, and a compact, walkable city that genuinely works.

The honest trade-off: Bilbao is rainy by Spanish standards. The Atlantic coast brings genuine northern weather — winters are wet, grey, and cool by Iberian standards. Expats who need sunshine as a priority should look south. But for those who prioritise quality of life, food, culture, and a genuine connection to one of Spain's most distinctive regional cultures, Bilbao is exceptional.

Cost of living is moderate — lower than Madrid and Barcelona, higher than Valencia or Seville. Rent for a one-bedroom apartment runs approximately €800–1,100 per month.

San Sebastián — the food capital of Europe

San Sebastián (Donostia in Basque) is small — under 200,000 people — but punches far above its weight in terms of food, culture, and quality of life. Its pintxos bar scene is world-famous, and the city has an extraordinary concentration of Michelin-starred restaurants per capita.

It is, however, one of the more expensive cities in Spain by cost of living — property and rent are high, and the city's premium reputation is reflected in prices. Expats on tight budgets will find San Sebastián challenging. For those with the means, it offers an incomparable lifestyle.

Alicante — the British expat hub with great value

Alicante is one of the most popular destinations for British expats in Spain, and for straightforward reasons: excellent weather (300+ sunny days), an international airport with direct UK routes, a large and well-established English-speaking community, affordable living costs, and a coastal lifestyle that delivers exactly what most British expats are seeking.

Rent in Alicante is among the more affordable in Spain's coastal cities: a one-bedroom apartment in a central or coastal neighbourhood costs approximately €600–900 per month. For digital nomads, the DNV income requirement of €2,849 per month provides a very comfortable standard of living in Alicante.

Las Palmas, Gran Canaria — Atlantic warmth and genuine diversity

Las Palmas de Gran Canaria has become one of the most prominent digital nomad destinations in Europe. Its year-round stable climate (18–25°C throughout the year), excellent beaches, affordable cost of living, and fast-growing international community make it compelling. The Canary Islands' location — closer to Africa than to mainland Spain geographically — gives Las Palmas a unique cosmopolitan feel distinct from anywhere else in Spain.

Rent for a one-bedroom apartment in a good neighbourhood of Las Palmas runs approximately €700–1,000 per month. Coworking spaces are plentiful and well-regarded. Fibre broadband is excellent. And the city has good air connections to mainland Spain, the UK, Germany, and other European hubs.
