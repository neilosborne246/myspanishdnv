Cost of Living

Cheapest cities to live in Spain — where your money goes furthest

Spain is already affordable by European standards. But choose wisely and your income goes extraordinarily far. This guide ranks Spain's best-value cities with real cost data for 2026.

By the My Spanish DNV team

Published: July 2026 Updated: regularly Read time: 11 min Category: Cost of Living

One of the most powerful advantages of moving to Spain on the Digital Nomad Visa is the gap between the income requirement and what life actually costs. The DNV minimum income of €2,849 per month provides a comfortable lifestyle in almost every Spanish city — and in the cheaper cities covered in this guide, it provides an outright excellent one. Understanding where your money goes furthest is one of the most valuable things you can do before choosing where to base yourself in Spain.

How to think about cost of living in Spain

Cost of living in Spain varies substantially by city, by neighbourhood within a city, and by lifestyle. The major driver is rent — it accounts for between 40% and 55% of most expats' monthly outgoings, and it varies by a factor of nearly three between the cheapest and most expensive Spanish cities. Other costs — food, utilities, transport, social life — are more uniform across Spain, though they track local income levels to some degree.

The framework we use when assessing Spanish cities for affordability:

  • Rent — monthly cost of a one-bedroom apartment in a pleasant but not premium central neighbourhood
  • Food — supermarket spend plus eating out two to three times per week
  • Transport — monthly public transport pass; car costs where relevant
  • Utilities — electricity, water, gas
  • Internet — fibre broadband (excellent throughout Spain)
  • Social life — coffees, drinks, weekend activities

Murcia — Spain's most affordable major city

Murcia is the cheapest major city to live in Spain. With a population of around 450,000, it is large enough to offer a genuine city experience — university life, good restaurants and tapas bars, cultural events, adequate shopping — without the premiums that come with Spain's coastal tourist cities or its largest urban centres.

Rent in Murcia is remarkably affordable: a one-bedroom apartment in a pleasant central neighbourhood typically costs €600–750 per month. Utilities (electricity, water, gas) run approximately €80–110 per month. Internet is fast and cheap at €30–45 per month for fibre. Food is extremely affordable — the menu del día (three-course weekday lunch with wine or water) costs €9–12 in most local restaurants, and supermarket bills for one person run €200–280 per month if you cook at home regularly.

Murcia is inland and warm — one of the sunniest regions in Spain, with summers that can be hot (regularly above 38°C in July and August) and very mild winters. It is approximately an hour from the coast (both the Mediterranean and the Mar Menor lagoon), making beach weekends easy. Alicante airport is around 45 minutes away, providing European flight connections.

The expat community in Murcia city is smaller than on the coast, but the wider Murcia region — particularly the Costa Cálida and Torrevieja areas — has a substantial British and Northern European community. For expats who want affordability, warmth, and a genuine Spanish experience without tourist infrastructure, Murcia is outstanding value.

Alicante — coastal affordability with a large expat community

Alicante offers something Murcia cannot: immediate coastal access, a large and well-established English-speaking expat community, and an international airport with direct connections to the UK, Germany, the Netherlands, and many other European countries. It is also very affordable — rent for a one-bedroom apartment in a central or beachside neighbourhood runs €650–850 per month.

For digital nomads on the DNV who want coastal living, a well-organised expat community, and low costs, Alicante is one of the strongest choices in Spain. The city has good coworking options, excellent fibre connectivity, and enough daily amenity that you rarely need to travel far for anything.

Valencia — the best value major city

Valencia deserves its reputation as the best overall value among Spain's major cities. It is significantly larger and better-connected than Murcia or Alicante — the third-largest city in Spain, with an international airport, direct connections to Madrid and Barcelona by fast train, and a comprehensive city infrastructure including metro, trams, and extensive cycling infrastructure.

Rent runs €700–1,000 per month for a one-bedroom apartment in a desirable neighbourhood — more than Murcia, but still less than half the cost of equivalent housing in London, Amsterdam, or Munich. The total cost of living for a single person in Valencia is approximately €1,100–1,500 per month including rent, food, utilities, and social activities.

For DNV holders, Valencia is perhaps the ideal base: the income requirement of €2,849 per month provides approximately €1,300–1,700 of surplus after all living costs, enough to save substantially while enjoying an excellent quality of life.

Seville — culture and warmth at a genuine price

Seville offers extraordinary cultural richness at a price that rivals the cheaper inland cities. Rent for a one-bedroom apartment in a pleasant Seville neighbourhood (Triana, Nervión, Los Remedios) runs approximately €700–950 per month. Food is excellent and cheap — tapas culture in Seville is genuinely world-class and remarkably affordable, with many bars still offering free tapas with a drink.

The city's summer heat (regularly exceeding 40°C in July and August) is the main trade-off. Many expats treat this as an opportunity to explore the cooler coast during peak summer, returning to Seville in September when the temperatures moderate and the city comes alive again for the autumn season.

Granada, Salamanca, Oviedo, Valladolid — the affordable inland options

Granada is a university city with extraordinary cultural heritage (the Alhambra palace, flamenco's Sacromonte neighbourhood) and very low living costs. Rent runs €600–800, and the student population keeps food and social costs low. The surrounding Sierra Nevada offers skiing in winter and hiking in summer.

Salamanca is one of Spain's most beautiful cities, dominated by its ancient university and sandstone architecture. Rent is €550–750 per month and the quality of life is high. It is, however, inland and somewhat colder in winter than southern cities.

Oviedo in Asturias is green, clean, relatively rainy (Atlantic climate), and extremely affordable. It has an excellent quality of life and very low costs — rent for a one-bedroom flat runs €550–750. It is a less obvious choice for expats who prioritise sunshine.

Valladolid and Zaragoza are both large, well-connected cities (good AVE high-speed rail links) with low costs and solid infrastructure. Neither has a large expat community, which makes them attractive for those seeking genuine integration into Spanish life.

Food costs across Spain

One of Spain's great affordability advantages is the quality and price of food. The menú del día — a three-course lunch served weekdays in most Spanish restaurants, typically including a starter, main, dessert, bread, and a drink — costs between €10 and €15 across most Spanish cities. This is genuinely excellent value for a sit-down meal.

Supermarket bills for one person who cooks at home regularly run approximately €200–300 per month. Fresh produce, meat, fish, and dairy are all competitively priced, and markets (mercados) offer exceptional fresh ingredients. Spain's large supermarket chains (Mercadona, Lidl, Carrefour, Dia) are competitive and well-stocked.

Utilities and internet

Utilities (electricity, water, and gas or heating depending on the property) typically cost €80–120 per month for a one-bedroom apartment in Spain. Summer air conditioning can push electricity bills higher in southern cities — budget €120–150 in the hottest months if you use air conditioning heavily.

Internet is one of Spain's genuine strengths. Spain has one of the best fibre broadband networks in Europe, with coverage extending well beyond major cities into smaller towns and villages. A residential fibre package offering 300–600 Mbps typically costs €30–50 per month. This is outstanding infrastructure for digital nomads — fast, reliable, and cheap. Coworking spaces in larger cities typically charge €100–250 per month for a dedicated desk, or €15–30 per day for drop-in access.

How the DNV income requirement plays out in practice

Spain's Digital Nomad Visa requires a minimum income of €2,849 per month — set at 200% of Spain's minimum wage. Here is how that income plays out across different cities:

💡

DNV income vs. living costs — the gap is significant

In Murcia or Salamanca, total monthly costs for a single person run €900–1,200. On the DNV minimum income of €2,849, that leaves €1,600–1,950 per month after all expenses — enough to save substantially, travel frequently, or simply enjoy Spain's lifestyle without financial stress. Even in Valencia, the surplus comfortably exceeds €1,000 per month.

This gap between the DNV income requirement and actual living costs in Spain's affordable cities is one of the most compelling financial arguments for the visa. For professionals earning above the minimum — which most DNV applicants do — the surplus grows further. In cities like Murcia, Alicante, or Valencia, an income of €4,000–5,000 per month creates a lifestyle that would require twice or three times that in London, Amsterdam, or Zurich.

Cost figures in this article are estimates based on typical market conditions in mid-2026. Rent in particular can vary significantly by neighbourhood, property type, and whether the apartment is furnished. Always research current listings before making decisions.
