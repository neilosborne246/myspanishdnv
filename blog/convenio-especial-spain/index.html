Convenio Especial Spain — how expats access public healthcare

Spain's public healthcare system is one of the best in the world. The Convenio Especial lets legal residents who aren't covered through work join the Sistema Nacional de Salud for around €60 a month — but there are conditions, a waiting period, and some important gaps in coverage to understand.

By the My Spanish DNV team

Published: July 2026 Updated: regularly Read time: 9 min Category: Health & Insurance

One of the first questions people ask when planning a move to Spain on the Digital Nomad Visa (DNV) or the Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV) is: what happens with healthcare? Private health insurance is mandatory for both visas during the application process — and generally for at least the first year of residence. But over time, many expats ask whether they can access Spain's excellent public healthcare system instead of — or alongside — their private cover.

The answer is yes, via an arrangement called the Convenio Especial de prestación de asistencia sanitaria — usually shortened to simply the Convenio Especial. This guide explains what it is, who qualifies, how much it costs, how to apply, and what it actually covers.

What is the Convenio Especial?

The Convenio Especial is a voluntary subscription scheme managed by Spain's social security administration — the Instituto Nacional de la Seguridad Social (INSS) — that allows legal residents to access the Sistema Nacional de Salud (SNS), Spain's public healthcare system, even when they are not entitled to it through standard routes.

Under the standard system, entitlement to public healthcare in Spain flows from one of three sources: employment (you and your employer pay Social Security contributions), self-employment as an autónomo (you pay your own contributions), or certain benefit entitlements such as unemployment or pension status. If none of these apply — which is the case for many DNV or NLV holders who are not working in Spain — you are not automatically entitled to public healthcare. The Convenio Especial fills that gap.

In practice, it means paying a fixed monthly sum to the INSS, in exchange for which you receive a tarjeta sanitaria (health card) and full access to SNS services in your region of Spain.

Who qualifies for the Convenio Especial?

To be eligible, you must meet all of the following conditions:

  • Legal residence in Spain: you must hold a valid residency permit or registration — a TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) or equivalent.
  • At least one year of legal residence: you must have been legally and continuously resident in Spain for a minimum of twelve months before applying. This is the most significant barrier for newly arrived expats.
  • Not already entitled through another route: you cannot use the Convenio Especial if you are already entitled to public healthcare through employment, autónomo status, or a benefit claim. It is specifically for those without another entitlement.
  • Not covered by a European Health Insurance Card (EHIC): EU/EEA nationals using an EHIC for emergency cover are not the target group for the Convenio Especial — the scheme is intended for ongoing, permanent residency situations.
💡

The one-year waiting period

The twelve-month legal residence requirement means that newly arrived DNV or NLV holders cannot immediately access the Convenio Especial. Private health insurance meeting the visa conditions remains essential for at least the first year — and often beyond, given processing times.

How much does the Convenio Especial cost?

The monthly contribution rates for 2026 are set by the Spanish government and are as follows:

Age group Monthly contribution Annual cost
Under 65 approx. €60/month approx. €720/year
65 and over approx. €157/month approx. €1,884/year

Each applicant pays their own contribution — dependants are not automatically included and must apply and pay separately. The contributions are paid directly to the INSS, typically by direct debit from a Spanish bank account.

Compared to comprehensive private health insurance in Spain — which can run to €100–200 per month or more depending on age and coverage — the Convenio Especial is extremely cost-effective for under-65s. For those aged 65 and over, private insurance often becomes very expensive, making the Convenio Especial a genuinely attractive alternative.

What does the Convenio Especial cover?

Once accepted onto the Convenio Especial, you receive access to the same services as any other SNS patient. The coverage includes:

  • GP (médico de cabecera) consultations at your assigned health centre (centro de salud)
  • Hospital treatment and surgery, including emergency inpatient care
  • Specialist referrals (always via your GP, not direct access)
  • Emergency care — including ambulance services
  • Prescription medications at the subsidised rate (typically 40–60% of the retail price, with some free for pensioners)
  • Mental health services (via GP referral)
  • Maternity care and paediatric services

What the Convenio Especial does NOT cover

There are some notable exclusions that catch people off guard:

  • Dental treatment: routine dental care is almost entirely outside the SNS. Emergency extractions may be covered, but fillings, crowns, implants, and orthodontics are not. Most residents maintain a separate dental insurance policy or pay privately for dental work.
  • Optical care: eye tests, glasses, and contact lenses are not covered by the SNS. Some regions subsidise spectacles for children, but adults pay privately.
  • Cosmetic procedures: reconstructive surgery following injury may be covered in some circumstances, but elective cosmetic procedures are not.
  • Some specialist medicines: certain high-cost treatments may not be available via the SNS or may require prior authorisation.

Many expats on the Convenio Especial supplement it with a basic dental plan — available in Spain from as little as €15–25 per month — to fill the most significant gap.

How to apply for the Convenio Especial

Applications are made in person at your local INSS office (Centro de Atención e Información de la Seguridad Social — CAISS). You can locate your nearest INSS office via the official Social Security website or by searching "INSS oficina" followed by your city or postal code. Appointments are generally required and can be booked online or by telephone.

Documents you will need

  • Your valid NIE (Número de Identificación de Extranjero)
  • Your current TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) — the physical residency card
  • Your empadronamiento certificate, dated within the last three months, showing you have been registered at your address for at least one year
  • Completed Form TA.1 — the application form for voluntary Social Security health coverage (solicitud de alta en el Convenio Especial de prestación de asistencia sanitaria)
  • Originals and photocopies of all documents
  • Spanish bank account details for direct debit set-up
📋

Download Form TA.1 in advance

Form TA.1 is available to download from the Spanish Social Security website (sede.seg-social.gob.es). Completing it before your appointment saves time. The form is in Spanish — if you are not confident with Spanish, have it translated or completed by an adviser before your appointment.

After your application is submitted and approved, you will receive a Social Security number (Número de Seguridad Social or NSS) if you do not already have one, and subsequently a health card for your region. Processing typically takes several weeks. You will then register with a local health centre (centro de salud) to be assigned a GP.

Convenio Especial vs private health insurance

These are not mutually exclusive — some expats hold both. However, it is worth understanding the key differences before deciding which route suits your situation.

Feature Convenio Especial (SNS) Private health insurance
Cost (under 65) ~€60/month ~€80–200+/month (age/cover dependent)
Waiting period 1 year legal residence required Available immediately
GP access Assigned local GP Choice of GP from insurer network
Specialist access Via GP referral only Often direct access
Waiting times Can be weeks or months for non-urgent care Generally faster for elective procedures
Dental cover Not included Often included or available as add-on
Visa requirement Does not satisfy visa insurance requirement Can satisfy visa insurance requirement

The SNS is genuinely excellent for serious and acute medical care — hospital treatment, surgery, cancer care, and maternity services are of very high quality across most of Spain. Where it can be slower is for non-urgent specialist consultations, elective procedures, and certain diagnostics, where waiting lists in some regions can be lengthy.

Convenio Especial vs Social Security contributions as autónomo

If you are self-employed in Spain and registered as an autónomo, you do not need the Convenio Especial. Autónomos pay monthly Social Security contributions as part of their registration — currently starting at a minimum of around €200 per month under the 2023 quota reform — and those contributions entitle them to full SNS healthcare access. The Convenio Especial is not the right route for autónomos; it is for those outside the Social Security contribution system entirely.

DNV holders and the Convenio Especial — a practical note

For Digital Nomad Visa holders, the picture is slightly nuanced. The DNV requires you to hold comprehensive private health insurance at the point of application — covering the full period of the visa, without copayments, and meeting a minimum coverage threshold. That private insurance remains a practical necessity for the first year or more.

After your first year of legal residence in Spain, if you are not employed or registered as autónomo, you may in principle apply for the Convenio Especial. However, you should consider whether your visa renewal obligations still require you to hold private insurance. If renewal conditions still specify private insurance, switching to the Convenio Especial alone may create complications. Take advice from your immigration lawyer before changing your health cover arrangements.

In practice, many DNV holders — particularly those earning well above the minimum threshold of €2,849 per month — maintain private insurance throughout their DNV period for the speed and convenience it offers, and consider the Convenio Especial only if they move onto long-term residency or naturalisation pathways.

Do not cancel private insurance prematurely

Cancelling private health insurance before you are accepted onto the Convenio Especial — and before confirming it satisfies any outstanding visa conditions — could leave you without compliant health cover and potentially jeopardise your renewal. Always confirm your position with your immigration lawyer before making changes.
