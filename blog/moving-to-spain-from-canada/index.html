Moving to Spain

Moving to Spain from Canada — visas, documents and settling in

More Canadians than ever are choosing Spain for its lifestyle, climate, and European access. Here's the complete 2026 guide to making the move legally — including what to do about your provincial health coverage, CRA tax obligations, and RCMP background check.

By the My Spanish DNV team

Published: July 2026 Updated: regularly Read time: 11 min Category: Moving to Spain

Canada and Spain may sit at opposite ends of the Atlantic, but they are closer in spirit than geography suggests. Spain offers Canadians winter sunshine, affordable cities, excellent food, and a pace of life that contrasts sharply with the frenetic rhythms of Toronto, Vancouver, or Calgary. For Canadian remote workers and freelancers, Spain's Digital Nomad Visa has made the dream of living in Europe a genuinely practical option.

This guide covers everything Canadian citizens need to know about moving to Spain in 2026 — from the visa process and required documents to tax obligations, provincial health insurance, and practical considerations for working with Canadian clients from Spanish time zones.

The 90-day Schengen rule for Canadians

Canadian citizens can enter Spain (and the wider Schengen Area) without a visa for short stays. However, the Schengen 90/180 rule restricts visa-free visits to a maximum of 90 days in any rolling 180-day period across all 26 Schengen countries combined.

This matters because many Canadians initially plan a "trial run" of 90 days in Spain before committing to a move. That is fine as a way of testing the waters — but once you decide to stay longer, you must obtain a long-stay national visa before your 90-day allowance is exhausted. You cannot legally extend your tourist status into residency from within Spain.

Visa options for Canadians moving to Spain

Spain Digital Nomad Visa (DNV)

The DNV is Spain's dedicated visa for remote workers and freelancers earning income from sources outside Spain. It was introduced in 2023 under Spain's Startups Law and has proven particularly popular with Canadian tech professionals, designers, consultants, and writers who work for Canadian companies or global clients.

The DNV provides an initial one-year visa that converts to a three-year residence permit on arrival in Spain. After three years, it can be renewed for a further two years. The 2026 income requirement is €2,849 per month for a single applicant. Families bringing dependants need an additional 75% of the base amount per dependant. Employed workers on the DNV may qualify for Beckham Law — Spain's 24% flat income tax rate — for up to six years.

Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV)

For Canadians who have reached retirement or have significant passive income and do not need to work in Spain, the NLV is the appropriate route. No employment or freelance work is permitted. The income threshold is linked to the Spanish IPREM indicator and is generally higher than the DNV threshold, reflecting the absence of work income.

Student visa

Canadians studying at a Spanish institution — whether a university degree, language course, or professional programme — need a student visa. Limited paid work is permitted in most cases.

The DNV is the standout option for Canadian remote workers

If you work for a Canadian company or serve Canadian clients remotely, the Digital Nomad Visa was built for your situation. It lets you continue your existing work while building legal residency in Spain — with a clear path to long-term permanent residency after five years.

Required documents — the RCMP criminal record check

The most Canada-specific document required for the Spain DNV application is the RCMP Criminal Record Check. This is a certified criminal record check issued by the Royal Canadian Mounted Police — not a local police check from your municipal force. The RCMP-issued certificate is what Spanish consulates require.

The Hague Apostille on your RCMP certificate

Once you have the RCMP certificate, it must have a Hague Apostille attached to authenticate it for use in Spain. In Canada, the Apostille is issued by Global Affairs Canada (Affaires mondiales Canada). The RCMP certificate must be submitted to Global Affairs Canada for apostillisation — allow 4–10 weeks for the combined process, as both the RCMP and Global Affairs Canada have their own processing queues. Expedited services are sometimes available but not always guaranteed.

Sworn Spanish translation

All documents not in Spanish must be accompanied by a sworn Spanish translation (traducción jurada) performed by a translator certified by the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs. This applies to your RCMP certificate, medical certificate, and any other supporting documents in English or French. Several Canada-based translators hold this certification and can work remotely.

Full document checklist for Canadians

  • Valid Canadian passport (minimum 6 months validity beyond intended stay in Spain)
  • Completed DNV application form
  • RCMP Criminal Record Check with Hague Apostille from Global Affairs Canada
  • Medical certificate confirming absence of notifiable conditions, with Hague Apostille
  • Sworn Spanish translation of all documents
  • Proof of income: employment contract, recent payslips, or certified accounts showing at least €2,849/month
  • Private health insurance covering Spain (minimum €30,000 coverage)
  • Proof of accommodation in Spain
  • Passport photographs to Spanish specifications

Canada-Spain tax treaty and CRA obligations

Canada and Spain have a comprehensive Convention for the Avoidance of Double Taxation. This treaty allocates taxing rights between the two countries and provides relief from being taxed twice on the same income. It is an important document for any Canadian who continues to receive Canadian-source income — such as employment income from a Canadian employer, Canadian investment income, or pension payments — after moving to Spain.

Canadian tax residency does not end automatically when you leave Canada

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) determines your Canadian tax residency using a facts-and-circumstances test. Key factors include whether you maintain a home in Canada, Canadian social ties, and provincial health insurance coverage. Cutting ties clearly and establishing Spanish tax residency is important — but take advice from a Canadian tax specialist who handles expatriate taxation before assuming you have left the Canadian tax net. This is not something to get wrong.

Once you establish Spanish tax residency, Spain's IRPF (income tax) applies to your worldwide income. The Canada-Spain double taxation convention provides the mechanism for coordinating tax obligations between the two countries. Your Spanish asesor fiscal (tax adviser) and your Canadian accountant should ideally coordinate on your cross-border position.

Provincial health insurance — what happens when you leave

This is one of the most practically important issues for Canadians moving to Spain. Provincial health insurance — OHIP in Ontario, MSP in British Columbia, AHCIP in Alberta, and so on — cancels when you have been absent from the province for an extended period. The threshold varies:

Province Typical coverage end
Ontario (OHIP) After 212 days (7 months) outside Ontario in a 12-month period
British Columbia (MSP) After 6 months absence; formally on de-registration or becoming non-resident
Alberta (AHCIP) After 6 months absence from Alberta
Quebec (RAMQ) Coverage extends for the first 6 months; terminates when you notify RAMQ or after extended absence

Once your provincial coverage lapses, you are fully reliant on your Spanish private health insurance. The DNV requires private health insurance as a condition of application in any case. Spanish private health insurance is of good quality and considerably more affordable than private coverage in Canada — budget approximately €80–200 per month depending on your age and level of cover.

Time zones — working for Canadian clients from Spain

One of the practical realities for Canadians continuing to work for Canadian clients is the time zone gap. Spain operates on Central European Time (CET, UTC+1) in winter and Central European Summer Time (CEST, UTC+2) in summer.

Canadian time zone Winter (CET) Summer (CEST)
Eastern (Toronto, Montreal) +6 hours +6 hours
Central (Winnipeg) +7 hours +7 hours
Mountain (Calgary) +8 hours +8 hours
Pacific (Vancouver) +9 hours +9 hours

The overlap between Spanish working hours and Eastern Canadian hours is workable — morning meetings in Toronto (9am) fall at 3pm in Spain. Pacific time is more challenging — a 9am Vancouver call lands at 6pm in Spain, which is manageable but means adjusting your Spanish working day to end later. Many Canadian remote workers in Spain shift their schedule to start early afternoon local time, preserving the overlap.

Banking in Spain as a Canadian

Major Spanish banks — Santander, BBVA, CaixaBank — are generally straightforward for Canadians to join, though the account-opening process requires your NIE, passport, and proof of Spanish address. Before leaving Canada, consider opening a Wise or Revolut account for competitive CAD-EUR currency conversion. Many Canadians also maintain their Canadian bank account throughout their time in Spain for receiving Canadian-source income.

Popular Spanish cities for Canadians

The Canadian community in Spain is concentrated in the larger cities, with Barcelona being the most popular choice — its international character, strong English-speaking expat community, and relatively familiar urban lifestyle make it an easy entry point. Valencia has grown significantly in popularity, offering a lower cost of living with a high quality of life and excellent direct transport links. Madrid offers a capital city experience with the broadest professional network. Seville and Málaga are popular choices for those seeking a warmer, more relaxed lifestyle with lower living costs than the big two.
