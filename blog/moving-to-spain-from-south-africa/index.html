Moving to Spain from South Africa — the complete 2026 guide

South African remote workers, freelancers, and tech professionals earning in USD, GBP, or EUR are ideally placed for Spain's Digital Nomad Visa. Here's everything you need to know about the visa process, SARS tax obligations, and making the move in 2026.

By the My Spanish DNV team

Published: July 2026 Updated: regularly Read time: 12 min Category: Moving to Spain

South Africa produces an extraordinary number of skilled remote workers, software developers, designers, and consultants who earn in hard currency — USD, GBP, EUR — while living in a country where the rand continues to face significant pressure. For these South Africans, Spain's Digital Nomad Visa represents a compelling option: the ability to live legally in Europe, spend in euros, and maintain existing client relationships and income streams.

This guide covers the full picture for South Africans considering Spain in 2026 — from visa options and SAPS police clearance through to SARS tax emigration, the foreign income exemption cap, and transferring money from South Africa to Spain.

What South Africans need to know about visiting Spain

South African passport holders have visa-free access to the Schengen Area for short stays — but only up to 90 days in any rolling 180-day period across all 26 Schengen countries combined. This is not 90 days in Spain alone: any time spent in France, Italy, Germany, Portugal, or any other Schengen country in the same 180-day window counts against this allowance.

For anyone genuinely planning to move to Spain rather than simply visit, the 90-day limit is not a viable long-term strategy. You must obtain a long-stay national visa before arriving in Spain. You cannot legally convert a tourist stay into residency from within Spain.

Visa options for South Africans moving to Spain

Spain Digital Nomad Visa (DNV)

The DNV is the standout option for South Africans who earn their income remotely from outside Spain. Introduced in 2023 under Spain's Startups Law, it is designed precisely for the profile of South Africa's growing remote workforce: skilled professionals earning in foreign currency, serving international clients or employed by companies outside Spain.

The DNV provides an initial one-year visa, which converts into a three-year residence permit on arrival in Spain. It can be renewed for a further two years, giving a total of up to five years of legal residency — enough to qualify for long-term EU residency. The 2026 income requirement is €2,849 per month for a single applicant, with 75% of this figure added per dependant. The rand-to-euro exchange rate means South Africans earning USD or GBP meet this threshold comfortably in many cases.

Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV)

For South Africans with sufficient passive income or savings who do not need to work in Spain, the NLV is an option. No employment or freelance work is permitted on this visa. The income threshold is higher than the DNV and the visa does not permit work — making it less suitable for most working-age South Africans.

Student visa

South Africans studying at Spanish institutions — universities, language schools — need a student visa. Limited paid work is generally permitted alongside study.

The DNV is particularly strong for SA tech and creative professionals

South Africa has a highly skilled developer, designer, and consultant community earning in foreign currency. The DNV income threshold of €2,849/month is readily achievable for most of these professionals, and the visa gives immediate access to the entire Schengen Area alongside legal Spanish residency.

SAPS Police Clearance Certificate — the key document

Spain requires a criminal record check from your country of residence for the DNV application. For South Africans, this is the South African Police Service (SAPS) Police Clearance Certificate, issued by the SAPS Criminal Record Centre in Pretoria.

This is not a standard fingerprint check from your local police station — it is a formal national clearance certificate issued centrally by SAPS. The process involves:

  1. Completing the SAPS application form and having your fingerprints taken at a SAPS station
  2. Submitting the application to the SAPS Criminal Record Centre in Pretoria (this can be done in person or via a registered agent)
  3. Receiving the SAPS Police Clearance Certificate — allow 4–8 weeks
  4. Obtaining a Hague Apostille on the certificate from the South African Department of International Relations and Cooperation (DIRCO)

South Africa is a signatory to the Hague Apostille Convention. DIRCO processes Apostilles for South African public documents. Allow an additional 2–4 weeks for the DIRCO Apostille process. Plan for 8–12 weeks in total for the SAPS certificate plus Apostille.

Sworn Spanish translation

All documents not in Spanish must be accompanied by a sworn Spanish translation (traducción jurada) by a translator certified by the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs. South African-based translators who hold this certification are available, as are international remote services.

Full document checklist

  • Valid South African passport (minimum 6 months validity beyond intended stay in Spain)
  • Completed DNV application form
  • SAPS Police Clearance Certificate with Hague Apostille from DIRCO
  • Medical certificate confirming absence of notifiable conditions, with Hague Apostille
  • Sworn Spanish translation of all documents
  • Proof of income showing at least €2,849/month (employment contract, payslips, certified accounts, or client contracts)
  • Private health insurance covering Spain (minimum €30,000 coverage)
  • Proof of accommodation in Spain
  • Passport photographs to Spanish specifications

SARS tax residency — what happens when you leave South Africa

South Africa has a residence-based tax system: South African tax residents are taxed on their worldwide income. This is one of the most important things for South Africans to understand before moving to Spain.

Get specialist South African tax advice before you move

South African tax emigration involves a deemed disposal of assets at exit (an exit tax), exchange control considerations, and careful coordination with the South African Revenue Service (SARS). The consequences of getting this wrong are significant. Always engage a qualified South African tax adviser who specialises in emigration before making the move.

Tax emigration from South Africa

To cease South African tax residency, you must notify SARS that you are no longer ordinarily resident in South Africa. SARS applies an exit tax — a deemed disposal of worldwide assets (excluding South African immovable property and certain other assets) at market value at the date of departure. Any gains on this deemed disposal are subject to capital gains tax in South Africa at exit. This is an important cost to model with your tax adviser before moving.

The foreign income exemption cap

Prior to 1 March 2020, South African tax residents could in many cases exempt foreign employment income from South African tax entirely. This exemption was capped from 1 March 2020. Currently, only the first R1.25 million per year of foreign employment income is exempt from South African income tax for South African tax residents who work outside South Africa for more than 183 days in a 12-month period (including more than 60 consecutive days). Income above R1.25 million remains taxable in South Africa.

This cap is the main reason many South Africans earning higher incomes from foreign clients are considering formal tax emigration — to cease South African tax residency entirely and pay tax only in Spain.

Transferring money from South Africa to Spain

South African residents are subject to exchange control regulations. In broad terms, South African residents have an annual single discretionary allowance and foreign investment allowance for transferring funds abroad — check current limits with your bank or a forex specialist, as these figures are updated by the Reserve Bank.

Once you have completed the tax emigration process and ceased South African tax residency, different rules apply to the transfer of your South African assets. A registered forex dealer or emigration specialist can guide you through the process.

For ongoing income transfers — converting monthly earnings from USD or GBP received in a foreign bank account into EUR for Spanish spending — services such as Wise and OFX offer significantly better exchange rates and lower fees than traditional banks. Many South Africans in Spain maintain a combination of a foreign currency account for receiving income and a Spanish bank account for day-to-day expenses.

Popular Spanish cities for South Africans

The South African community in Spain is growing rapidly, concentrated particularly in:

  • Barcelona — the most cosmopolitan Spanish city, with an established international community and excellent quality of life. Higher rents than other cities, but strong professional networks
  • Madrid — the capital, with the largest job market and the most diverse international community. Good direct flights to South Africa
  • Valencia — increasingly popular for its lower cost of living, warm climate, beaches, and fast-growing tech and digital community
  • Málaga — the Andalusian sun capital, with a growing expat community and international airport connections

Spanish people as a whole are warm and hospitable, and South Africans typically find the adjustment to Spanish culture — the social warmth, the food culture, the late hours — surprisingly natural.

Spanish Sephardic ancestry — is there a citizenship route?

Some South Africans have Sephardic Jewish ancestry — descendants of the Jewish communities expelled from Spain during the Inquisition of 1492. Spain operated a special naturalisation programme for Sephardic descendants between 2015 and 2019, which allowed qualifying applicants to obtain Spanish citizenship without renouncing their South African citizenship.

That specific programme is now closed. However, South Africans with documented Sephardic heritage should consult a Spanish immigration lawyer to understand whether any current or future pathway is available. For the majority of South Africans, the Digital Nomad Visa followed by the standard five-year residency route to permanent residency remains the most practical pathway.
