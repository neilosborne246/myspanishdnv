Spanish driving licence — what expats need to know in 2026

Becoming a Spanish resident changes your driving licence situation entirely. Whether you hold an EU, UK, US, or Australian licence, the rules differ — and the clock starts ticking from the date you register your residency.

By the My Spanish DNV team

Published: July 2026 Updated: regularly Read time: 10 min Category: Practical Guides

Many people who move to Spain on the Digital Nomad Visa (DNV) or the Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV) assume they can simply continue driving on their existing foreign licence indefinitely. This is a mistake. Spanish law imposes specific deadlines for exchanging or converting foreign licences once you become a resident, and the rules vary significantly depending on which country issued your licence.

The authority responsible for driving licences in Spain is the Dirección General de Tráfico (DGT). All licence exchanges, applications, and renewals go through the DGT, either at a provincial traffic office or through an authorised gestor (administrative agent).

This guide sets out the rules that apply to the most common licence-issuing countries for DNV and NLV applicants: the UK, EU/EEA member states, the US, Australia, Canada, and South Africa.

The key principle: residency changes your position

As a tourist or visitor in Spain, you can drive on your valid foreign licence for the duration of your stay. No issue. The situation changes entirely once you become a Spanish resident — specifically, once you hold a TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero), Spain's residency card for non-EU nationals.

From the date your TIE is issued, a clock starts. Non-EU residents have six months from the date on their TIE in which to either exchange their foreign licence for a Spanish one or pass the Spanish driving tests. After those six months, driving on the foreign licence as a Spanish resident is not legally permitted.

EU/EEA residents have different rules: their licences remain valid in Spain on an ongoing basis, but there are strong practical reasons — and eventually legal obligations — to exchange them for a Spanish licence over time.

EU/EEA driving licences in Spain

If you hold a driving licence issued by an EU or EEA member state (France, Germany, Netherlands, Italy, Ireland, Portugal, Sweden, etc.), your licence is mutually recognised across the EU and remains valid in Spain. You do not face the six-month deadline that applies to non-EU nationals.

However, there are practical and legal reasons to exchange your EU licence for a Spanish one over time:

  • Spain's point-based licence system (12 points) applies to all Spanish residents, regardless of which country issued their licence. Points are deducted from your record even if you hold a foreign EU licence.
  • If your EU licence expires while you are resident in Spain, you must renew it through the DGT — you cannot renew via your country of origin once you are a Spanish resident.
  • Hiring vehicles in Spain or other EU countries is generally straightforward with a current EU licence.

The exchange process for EU/EEA licences is administratively simple: present your licence, NIE, TIE, a passport photo, and a medical certificate at a DGT office. No test is required. The DGT fee is approximately €90.

UK driving licences in Spain — the post-Brexit position

Before Brexit, UK licences were treated as EU licences for driving purposes in Spain. That changed on 1 January 2021. The UK is now classified as a third country, and UK licences fall under non-EU rules.

The critical timeline for UK licence holders who become Spanish residents is:

  • 6 months from TIE date: you must exchange your UK licence for a Spanish one, or pass the Spanish driving tests.
  • After 6 months: driving on a UK licence as a Spanish resident is not permitted, regardless of how recently the UK licence was issued or renewed.

The UK–Spain bilateral exchange agreement

The good news for UK licence holders is that a bilateral driving licence exchange agreement between the UK and Spain is in force. This means that qualifying UK licence holders can exchange their licence for a Spanish one without taking the Spanish theory or practical test. The original UK licence is surrendered as part of the process and returned to the UK Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency (DVLA).

To exchange, you will need:

  • Your valid UK driving licence (photocard)
  • Your NIE and TIE
  • A valid medical certificate from a DGT-authorised medical centre (centro de reconocimiento de conductores)
  • A recent passport-size photograph
  • Completed DGT application form
  • Payment of the DGT fee (approximately €90)

The medical certificate costs approximately €20–40 and can be obtained quickly from an authorised medical centre — these are separate from GP surgeries and dedicated to driving licence medicals. Most cities have several.

Act before the 6-month deadline

Do not wait until the final weeks before the six-month deadline to begin the exchange process. DGT offices can be busy, and appointment availability varies by province. Start the process within the first 2–3 months of obtaining your TIE to give yourself comfortable margin.

US driving licences in Spain

The United States does not have a blanket bilateral driving licence exchange agreement with Spain. The default position for most US licence holders who become Spanish residents is that they must pass the full Spanish driving tests — both the theory and the practical — to obtain a Spanish licence once the six-month residency grace period expires.

A small number of individual US states have limited agreements with Spain, but these are the exception rather than the rule. Before assuming your state is covered, verify the current position with the DGT or a qualified Spanish gestor.

The Spanish driving theory test for US nationals

The DGT theory test consists of 30 multiple-choice questions. To pass, candidates must answer at least 27 correctly — a pass rate of 90%, which is substantially higher than many countries' testing standards. The test covers:

  • Spanish and EU road signs
  • Traffic rules and right-of-way regulations
  • Speed limits on different road types
  • Vehicle safety and technical requirements
  • First aid basics
  • Environmental driving considerations

The test is available in English. Most candidates prepare using the DGT's official online practice system or via a driving school (autoescuela). A reasonable preparation period is 4–8 weeks of focused study, with regular use of the practice test bank.

Australian, Canadian, and South African licences

Australia, Canada (most provinces), and South Africa do not have bilateral exchange agreements with Spain. Residents from these countries must take the full Spanish theory and practical tests after the six-month grace period.

The practical test in Spain takes place on a DGT test route in a vehicle provided by your autoescuela. The examiner accompanies you and assesses your driving to Spanish standards. Most non-EU candidates who have driven for many years still require some practice in Spain to familiarise themselves with local driving conventions before taking the test.

Spain's point-based licence system

All drivers in Spain — whether holding a Spanish licence or a recognised foreign licence — are subject to Spain's 12-point driving licence system. New drivers start with 12 points. Points are deducted for traffic offences, and if your total reaches zero, your licence is suspended. Drivers with a clean record can earn back points up to a maximum of 15 over time through driver awareness courses.

Common penalties include:

Offence Points lost
Using a mobile phone while driving 3 points
Not wearing a seatbelt 3 points
Speeding (20–30 km/h over limit) 2 points
Speeding (30–50 km/h over limit) 4 points
Drink-driving (over limit) 4–6 points
Dangerous driving / reckless driving 6 points

Costs — what to budget

The cost of obtaining a Spanish driving licence varies by route:

  • Exchange route (UK, EU, bilateral agreement countries): medical certificate (~€20–40) + DGT fee (~€90) = total approximately €110–130.
  • Full test route (US, Australia, Canada, South Africa etc.): medical certificate (~€20–40) + autoescuela theory preparation classes (~€150–300) + theory test fee + autoescuela practical lessons (~€40–60 per lesson, typically 10–20 lessons needed) + practical test fee = total approximately €600–1,200 depending on preparation needed and region.

Many autoescuelas offer package deals for foreign licence holders that include theory preparation, mock tests, and a set number of practical lessons. These packages often represent better value than paying à la carte.

Practical tips

  • Note the exact date on your TIE — this starts the six-month clock, not the date you arrived in Spain.
  • The DGT's official website (dgt.es) has an online appointment system for provincial traffic offices. Book early — popular offices have waiting times of several weeks.
  • A Spanish gestor (administrative agent) can handle the exchange paperwork on your behalf, which is useful if your Spanish is limited. Budget €50–100 for this service.
  • If your original country's licence is close to its expiry date, renew it before moving to Spain — you will need to present a valid licence for the exchange or test process.
  • Keep a photocopy of your existing licence before surrendering it during the exchange process.

Driving after the 6-month deadline without a Spanish licence is an offence

If you are stopped by the DGT or Guardia Civil after your six-month grace period has expired and you are still driving on a foreign licence, you face a fine and potentially having your vehicle impounded. Do not let the deadline pass — get your exchange or test completed in good time.
